about me

Before I got into software engineering, I spent years building actual businesses. I learned pretty quickly that a system only matters if it actually holds up in production, under real users and real load.

At 17, I started ClickForAyurveda, an e-commerce website achieving 25-45% in profit margins. I sourced products, built the brand, and handled all the digital marketing - Facebook ads, Instagram content, health blogs, the whole thing. But I spent most of my time analyzing unit economics: CAC vs LTV, retention rates, demand patterns. Eventually, competition ramped up, ad costs exploded, and the math stopped making sense. I made the call to shut it down. Not easy, but it was the right move.

I founded Dhani Agro Food Industries, ran a tofu production plant for 3 years. Managed the full production cycle and supplied 12 hotels every day. I figured out how to cut production costs from ₹160 to ₹100 per unit through observation, innovation and experimentation. I also got into product innovation & made kalakand (a tofu-based sweet) that originally cost ₹220/kg to make and sold for ₹350/kg. I reformulated it with gulkand and essences, which brought production cost to ₹240/kg but let us sell it at ₹400/kg. That pushed margins from 59% to 67% - an 8 percentage point jump. The daily work involved inventory planning, demand forecasting, supplier negotiations, and making sure quality stayed consistent.

That instinct for taking a system apart and rebuilding it properly is what pulled me into backend engineering. Today I build APIs with FastAPI, handle authentication with JWT and OAuth 2.0, and work with SQL/SQLAlchemy and Django to design the systems underneath a product rather than the storefront on top of it.

I finished my BCom In Banking and Finance with an 8.83 CGPA from SPPU university. Now I'm pursuing an MCA in AI & Machine Learning at Amity University with ongoing 8.32 CGPA. On the AI side, I'm going beyond just using models - I'm learning the fundamentals of PyTorch, NLP, and LLMs, and training small models from scratch to actually understand what's happening under the hood: embeddings, attention, gradients, the whole training loop.

Aligned to Whom?

Rejecting Moral Authority in the Age of Artificial Intelligence

A philosophy paper arguing that the dominant AI alignment paradigm rests on a flawed assumption: that human values are stable, coherent, and extractable into code. Drawing on cross-cultural psychology, neuroscience, phenomenology, and political philosophy, it argues that encoding a single moral framework into AI risks freezing one generation's contested worldview into permanent, globally-scaled infrastructure.

Key Ideas:

  • Shows human morality is culturally contingent and only partially accessible even to those who hold it
  • Proposes "Sovereign Proceduralism," distinguishing defensive alignment (preventing clear harms like violence, fraud, coercion) from ideological alignment (taking sides on religion, politics, philosophy)
  • Argues AI should enforce the former and actively avoid the latter, staying transparent about uncertainty and hospitable to disagreement
  • Concludes AI's role is to remain an honest participant in human moral conversation, not to serve as its arbiter
